# Part I: The Major Prophets

---

## 1. Isaiah (c. 740-700 BCE)

**Background.** Isaiah ben Amoz prophesied in Jerusalem during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. His book is the longest of the prophetic books. Modern scholarship generally divides it into Proto-Isaiah (ch. 1-39), Deutero-Isaiah (ch. 40-55), and Trito-Isaiah (ch. 56-66), though traditional scholars maintain single authorship.

### Key Prophecies

- **Isaiah 7:14** -- "A young woman/virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." The most debated verse in the Old Testament (see Unfulfilled section below).
- **Isaiah 9:1-7** -- A child born who will reign on David's throne forever, called "Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."
- **Isaiah 11:1-9** -- A branch from Jesse bringing an era of peace where wolves lie down with lambs.
- **Isaiah 40:3** -- "A voice cries in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the LORD.'" Applied in all four Gospels to John the Baptist.
- **Isaiah 42:1-9** -- The first Servant Song. Islamic tradition reads this as a prophecy of Muhammad.
- **Isaiah 44:28; 45:1** -- Names Cyrus the Great as the one who will rebuild Jerusalem.
- **Isaiah 52:13-53:12** -- The fourth Servant Song. Christians interpret this as Jesus's suffering and death; Jewish tradition understands it as Israel personified.
- **Isaiah 60-66** -- A glorified, restored Jerusalem where nations stream to it. Considered unfulfilled by most traditions.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- **Isaiah 1:16-17** -- "Wash yourselves, make yourselves clean... cease to do evil, learn to do good; seek justice, rescue the oppressed, defend the orphan, plead for the widow."
- **Isaiah 56:1-8** -- Inclusion of foreigners and eunuchs in the covenant community.
- **Isaiah 58:6-7** -- True fasting defined as loosing the bonds of injustice, feeding the hungry, sheltering the homeless.
- Strong condemnation of economic exploitation, bribery, and empty ritual worship divorced from ethical behavior (1:11-15).

### Religious Influence

- **Judaism:** More synagogue Haftara readings are drawn from Isaiah than any other prophetic book.
- **Christianity:** Called the "Fifth Gospel" by early church fathers. The most cited prophetic text in the New Testament. Central to Mariology (Virgin Birth), Christology (Suffering Servant), and the theology of Gentile inclusion.
- **Islam:** Muslim scholars claim Isaiah 42 prophesies Muhammad.

### Unfulfilled and Disputed Prophecies

- **Isaiah 7:14 -- The "Almah" Controversy.** The Hebrew `almah` means "young woman of marriageable age," not necessarily "virgin." The Greek Septuagint used `parthenos` (virgin), which Matthew 1:23 then cites. Jewish scholars since at least the 2nd century CE have argued this referred to a young woman in Isaiah's own time, a sign for King Ahaz in an immediate political crisis.
- **Isaiah 17:1 -- Damascus.** "Damascus will cease to be a city and will become a heap of ruins." Damascus was damaged by Tiglath-pileser III in 732 BCE but was never permanently destroyed. It is widely cited as the oldest continuously inhabited city in the world.
- **Isaiah 19 -- Egypt.** Prophecy that Egypt would become desolate, speak Hebrew, and worship YHWH alongside Assyria. Egypt never adopted Hebrew, and the prophesied highway between Egypt, Assyria, and Israel was never built.
- **Isaiah 60-66 -- Glorified Jerusalem.** The vision of eternal Jerusalem where mourning is abolished remains unfulfilled by any historical standard.

---

## 2. Jeremiah (c. 627-587 BCE)

**Background.** Jeremiah ben Hilkiah prophesied through the reigns of Josiah, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah, witnessing the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem. He dictated his prophecies to his scribe Baruch. Often called the "Weeping Prophet."

### Key Prophecies

- **Jeremiah 25:11-12; 29:10** -- Judah would serve Babylon for seventy years, after which Babylon would be punished.
- **Jeremiah 31:31-34** -- The New Covenant: God promises to write his law on hearts rather than stone. Directly quoted in Hebrews 8:8-12, making it foundational to Christian theology. The word "testament" in "New Testament" derives from the Latin translation of "covenant" here.
- **Jeremiah 33:15-17** -- A righteous branch from David's line, and David's dynasty will never lack a man on the throne.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- **Jeremiah 7:5-7** -- The Temple Sermon: justice for the alien, orphan, and widow, and not shedding innocent blood.
- **Jeremiah 22:3** -- Direct command to the king: do justice and righteousness, deliver the robbed, do no violence to aliens, fatherless, and widows.
- The New Covenant (31:31-34) represents Jeremiah's most radical contribution: law internalized, not externally coerced.

### Religious Influence

- **Judaism:** His concept of individual moral responsibility was enormously influential. His letters to the Babylonian exiles helped reframe Judaism as portable and non-Temple-dependent -- the foundation for rabbinic Judaism after 70 CE.
- **Christianity:** The New Covenant passage is the most quoted OT passage in the NT and provided the theological basis for the claim that Jesus inaugurated a new covenant.

### Unfulfilled and Disputed Prophecies

- **Jeremiah 36:30 -- Jehoiakim's Fate.** Predicted Jehoiakim would "have no one to sit on the throne of David," but Jehoiachin (his son) did briefly succeed him for three months.
- **Jeremiah 33:17 -- Unbroken Davidic Dynasty.** The Babylonian exile ended the historical Davidic line. Judaism anticipates fulfillment in a future Messiah; Christianity claims fulfillment in Jesus.
- **Jeremiah 43; 46 -- Nebuchadnezzar Conquering Egypt.** While Nebuchadnezzar campaigned in Egypt around 568 BCE, there is no record of Egypt being conquered, occupied for forty years, or devastated as described. A scholarly article at TheTorah.com titled "Nebuchadnezzar Fails to Conquer Egypt So Jeremiah's Prophecy Was Updated" analyzes this directly.

---

## 3. Ezekiel (c. 593-571 BCE)

**Background.** Ezekiel was a priest deported to Babylon in 597 BCE. He prophesied to the Jewish exiles from approximately 593 to 571 BCE.

### Key Prophecies

- **Ezekiel 1** -- The Chariot Vision (Merkabah): God enthroned on a mobile chariot. Foundation of Jewish mysticism.
- **Ezekiel 18** -- Individual moral responsibility: "The person who sins shall die. A child shall not suffer for the iniquity of a parent."
- **Ezekiel 26-28** -- Extended prophecy against Tyre (see Disputed section -- major controversy).
- **Ezekiel 37:1-14** -- The Valley of Dry Bones: scattered bones coming to life, representing the restoration of Israel.
- **Ezekiel 38-39** -- The Gog and Magog War: a future northern invasion miraculously defeated by God.
- **Ezekiel 40-48** -- Detailed vision of a future Temple, its measurements, and priestly regulations.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- **Chapter 18** -- The individual is morally responsible for their own sin. A righteous person who turns to evil will die; a wicked person who turns to righteousness will live.
- **Chapters 40-46** -- Only Zadokite priests may serve at the altar. Levites who followed idols are demoted. Rules on priestly garments, diet, marriage, and contact with the dead.
- **Chapters 3, 33** -- The "watchman" metaphor: leaders are responsible to warn their communities. Silence makes one complicit.

### Religious Influence

- **Judaism:** Individual moral responsibility was transformative for diaspora Judaism. The Merkabah vision seeded Jewish mysticism and eventually Kabbalah.
- **Christianity:** The most cited OT book in Revelation. The river of life (Ezekiel 47) reappears in Revelation 22. The four living creatures of Ezekiel 1 appear in Revelation 4.

### Unfulfilled and Disputed Prophecies

- **Ezekiel 26-28 -- The Tyre Prophecy.** Perhaps the most extensively debated "failed prophecy" in the OT. Ezekiel predicted Nebuchadnezzar would destroy Tyre, make it "a bare rock," never to be rebuilt. Nebuchadnezzar besieged Tyre for 13 years but the island city survived. **Critically:** Ezekiel 29:17-20 (written in 571 BCE, after the siege ended) states Nebuchadnezzar "received no payment" from Tyre. Most critical scholars read this as Ezekiel himself acknowledging the prophecy failed and offering a revised prediction.
- **Ezekiel 29-32 -- Egypt.** Predicted Egypt would become a "desolate wasteland" for forty years. There was no forty-year exile of Egyptians. Egypt continued as a functioning civilization for centuries.
- **Ezekiel 38-39 -- Gog and Magog.** No historically satisfying fulfillment. Revelation reuses the language (20:8) for its own end-times scenario.
- **Ezekiel 40-48 -- The Temple Vision.** Never built to these specifications. The Second Temple did not match Ezekiel's dimensions. Discrepancies between Ezekiel's Temple laws and the Mosaic Torah were so serious that the Talmud (Shabbat 13b) records debates about whether Ezekiel should even be in the canon.

## 4. Daniel (Traditional: 605-530 BCE / Critical: 167-164 BCE)

**Background.** The most controversial of the Major Prophets. The critical scholarly consensus is that the book was written during the Maccabean period (167-164 BCE), after Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrated the Jerusalem Temple. The philosopher Porphyry (~234-305 CE) first noted that the "prophecies" in Daniel 11 read like history up through 164 BCE and then suddenly become inaccurate. The book is classified as *vaticinium ex eventu* (prophecy after the fact) by critical scholars.

### Key Prophecies

- **Daniel 2** -- Nebuchadnezzar's statue dream: gold, silver, bronze, iron, clay-iron -- successive empires destroyed by a divine stone.
- **Daniel 7** -- Four Beasts and the "Son of Man" who receives an everlasting kingdom. Jesus's most frequent self-designation in the Gospels draws from this passage.
- **Daniel 9:24-27** -- The "Seventy Weeks": 490 years decreed, ending with the anointed one "cut off." Four major interpretive frameworks compete (see Disputed section).
- **Daniel 11** -- Narrates the Ptolemaic-Seleucid wars with remarkable accuracy through ~164 BCE, then loses accuracy precisely where Antiochus IV's actual death diverges from the prediction.
- **Daniel 12** -- Resurrection of the dead: "Many who sleep in the dust shall awake." One of the clearest OT references to individual bodily resurrection.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- **Daniel 1** -- Dietary faithfulness: refusing the king's non-kosher food. A model for Jewish dietary fidelity under foreign pressure.
- **Daniel 3** -- Refusal to worship idols even under death threat. Foundational for Jewish and Christian martyrology.
- **Daniel 6** -- Faithfulness in prayer regardless of civil law.
- Implicit theology of sovereign patience: remain faithful under oppression, trust in divine vindication.

### Religious Influence

- **Judaism:** Daniel's apocalyptic framework shaped all of Second Temple Judaism. Placed not among the Prophets (Nevi'im) but in the Writings (Ketuvim), reflecting ancient debates about its prophetic status.
- **Christianity:** "Son of Man" language pervades the Gospels. The Book of Revelation is incomprehensible without Daniel. End-times theology -- the Antichrist, the Great Tribulation, the mark of the beast -- is largely built on Daniel. In the 19th century, the Millerites used Daniel 8:14 to calculate Christ's return for October 22, 1844 -- the "Great Disappointment."
- **Islam:** Daniel appears in later Islamic literature as a recognized prophet.

### Unfulfilled and Disputed Prophecies

- **Daniel 11:40-45 -- Antiochus's Death.** Predicts Antiochus IV would die "between the sea and Jerusalem." He actually died in Persia (Ecbatana or Isfahan). This factual inaccuracy immediately after a sequence of accurate "predictions" is the primary evidence that the author lost access to reliable information at the point where events hadn't happened yet.
- **Daniel 12:11-12 -- Resurrection Timeline.** The expected resurrection and establishment of God's kingdom approximately 3.5 years after the Temple desecration (i.e., ~163-162 BCE) did not occur.
- **Daniel 9:24-27 -- The Seventy Weeks.** Four competing frameworks exist: (1) Maccabean preterist -- all fulfilled by 164 BCE, the "cut off" anointed one being high priest Onias III; (2) Christological -- the 69 weeks end with Jesus's crucifixion; (3) Dispensationalist -- a "gap" of the Church Age separates weeks 69 and 70; (4) Amillennial -- fulfilled entirely in the apostolic age. Centuries of debate have produced no consensus.

# Part II: The Twelve Minor Prophets

---

## 1. Hosea (c. 755-715 BCE)

**Background.** Hosea prophesied during the reigns of Uzziah through Hezekiah of Judah and Jeroboam II of Israel. God commanded him to marry Gomer, an unfaithful woman, as a living metaphor for Israel's unfaithfulness to God.

### Key Prophecies

- Israel would be destroyed and exiled to Assyria for idolatry (9:3, 11:5).
- Israel would "return and seek the LORD their God, and David their king" in the last days (3:5).
- Hosea 11:1 ("Out of Egypt I called my son") is applied in Matthew 2:15 to the return of Jesus from Egypt.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Return to exclusive monotheism; no worship of Baal.
- Practice of *hesed* (lovingkindness) as the supreme religious virtue.
- **Hosea 6:6** -- "I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice" -- directly quoted by Jesus in Matthew 9:13 and 12:7.

### Religious Influence

Hosea pioneered the concept of God as a loving, faithful spouse grieved by betrayal, shaping covenant theology for all later prophets and the New Testament image of the Church as Bride of Christ.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Hosea 3:5** -- The literal return of ethnic Israel to worship a restored Davidic king has not occurred.
- **Hosea 1:10-11** -- Reunification of Judah and Israel under one head has never taken place.
- **Hosea 2:18** -- A covenant of peace with animals, abolishing warfare. Universal peace has not occurred.

---

## 2. Joel (Date Uncertain)

**Background.** Joel's dating is contested -- either c. 835 BCE or post-exilic (~400 BCE). The book opens with a devastating locust plague used as backdrop for broader eschatological warnings.

### Key Prophecies

- The "Day of the Lord" -- cosmic judgment with darkened sun and moon (2:1-11).
- **Joel 2:28-32** -- The outpouring of the Holy Spirit upon all flesh: "your sons and daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions."
- All nations judged in the "Valley of Jehoshaphat" (3:1-3).

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Communal fasting, weeping, and repentance -- "Rend your hearts and not your garments" (2:13).
- Internal observance over ritual performance.

### Religious Influence

Peter quoted Joel 2:28-32 at Pentecost (Acts 2:16-21), declaring it fulfilled. This passage became the theological foundation for Christian pneumatology and the charismatic/Pentecostal movements.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Joel 2:30-31** -- Blood, fire, columns of smoke, sun to darkness, moon to blood. None of these astronomical signs occurred at Pentecost.
- **Joel 3:12-17** -- All nations gathered for final judgment, universal war followed by cosmic transformation. Has never occurred.

---

## 3. Amos (c. 760-750 BCE)

**Background.** A shepherd from Tekoa in Judah, Amos is generally regarded as the earliest writing prophet. He prophesied against the northern kingdom during Jeroboam II's prosperous reign.

### Key Prophecies

- Oracles against eight nations, each indicted for specific ethical crimes (ch. 1-2).
- The Day of the LORD as darkness, not light -- a radical reversal of popular expectation (5:18-20).
- A famine "not of bread, but of hearing the words of the LORD" (8:11).
- Restoration of David's fallen tent and the return of the exiles (9:11-15).

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Justice in the courts: no bribery, no denying the poor a hearing (5:12, 15).
- Economic justice: no exploitation, no dishonest scales (8:4-6).
- **Amos 5:21-24** -- God rejects feasts, offerings, and music when unaccompanied by justice: "Let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."

### Religious Influence

Amos is the Bible's pre-eminent prophet of social justice. Martin Luther King Jr. quoted Amos 5:24 in the "I Have a Dream" speech. The apostle James cited Amos 9:11 at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15) to justify Gentile inclusion.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Amos 9:11-15** -- Israel permanently replanted in its land, "never again to be uprooted." Literal, permanent restoration remains contested.
- **Amos 9:13** -- "The mountains shall drip sweet wine, and all the hills shall flow with it." Hyperbolic agricultural abundance has never literally occurred.

---

## 4. Obadiah (Date Uncertain)

**Background.** The shortest book of the Hebrew Bible -- 21 verses. Entirely devoted to judgment against Edom for its treachery when Jerusalem fell.

### Key Prophecies

- Complete annihilation of Edom: "There shall be no survivor of the house of Esau" (v. 18).
- The Day of the LORD upon all nations (v. 15).
- Israelite territorial expansion to encompass Edom and neighboring lands (vv. 19-20).

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Do not gloat over your brother's misfortune (v. 12).
- Do not loot in a day of calamity (v. 13).
- Pride and self-sufficiency are condemned as root sins.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Obadiah 15-21** -- The full territorial expansion and theocratic kingdom ruled from Zion have never occurred. The Edomites were displaced by the Nabateans (~5th century BCE) and eventually absorbed, but the broader vision of universal judgment and Israelite sovereignty remains unfulfilled.

---

## 5. Jonah (c. 780 BCE Historically; Book Composed Later)

**Background.** Most critical scholars regard the Book of Jonah as a didactic narrative or satire composed in the 5th-4th century BCE. It is unique among the Minor Prophets as a narrative *about* a prophet rather than a collection of oracles.

### Key Prophecies

- **Jonah 3:4** -- "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" This prophecy demonstrably did not come true within the narrative itself -- Nineveh repented and God relented.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Repentance is possible for any community, regardless of how wicked.
- Divine mercy is not ethnocentric.
- The book critiques those who would restrict divine grace to their own group.

### Religious Influence

- **Christianity:** Jesus cited the "sign of Jonah" as a type of his death and resurrection (Matthew 12:39-41).
- **Judaism:** Read during the Yom Kippur afternoon service as a meditation on repentance and universal divine concern.
- **Islam:** Jonah (Yunus) is one of the few prophets explicitly named in the Quran multiple times.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **The sole prophecy of Jonah was explicitly unfulfilled by design.** The narrative presents this as the point: God's mercy overrides prediction. This makes Jonah technically a "failed prophet" by Deuteronomy 18:22's criterion, and the paradox has long intrigued scholars. The book is intentionally subversive literature.

---

## 6. Micah (c. 738-700 BCE)

**Background.** A contemporary of Isaiah, Micah came from rural Moresheth in Judah.

### Key Prophecies

- Destruction of Samaria (1:6-7) -- fulfilled by Assyria in 722/721 BCE.
- **Micah 5:2** -- Bethlehem will produce the ruler of Israel "whose goings out are from of old." Universally applied by Christians to Jesus's birth.
- **Micah 4:1-4** -- Nations streaming to Jerusalem; swords beaten into plowshares; universal peace. This inspired the sculpture at the United Nations.
- **Micah 6:8** -- "What does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?" Arguably the most compressed ethical statement in the Hebrew Bible.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Justice for the poor; condemnation of rulers who exploit their people.
- Rejection of dishonest weights and measures (6:10-11).
- Walking humbly with God as the essence of religious life.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Micah 4:1-4** -- Universal peace, nations flowing to Jerusalem for instruction, cessation of all warfare. Obviously has not occurred.
- **Micah 5:10-15** -- God will eliminate all weapons, destroy cities, eliminate sorcery. Has not taken place.

---

## 7. Nahum (c. 663-612 BCE)

**Background.** Nahum's book is entirely devoted to the coming destruction of Nineveh, capital of Assyria. Many scholars believe it was composed very close to 612 BCE, possibly as ex eventu writing.

### Key Prophecies

- Nineveh will be besieged, fall, and its defenses shattered (2:1-10).
- Total and permanent destruction (3:19).

### Religious Influence

Nahum stands in deliberate tension with Jonah: while Jonah depicts God's mercy toward Nineveh, Nahum depicts God's judgment. Together they form a canonical meditation on divine patience and divine justice.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- The primary prophecies about Nineveh's fall were fulfilled in 612 BCE. However, archaeological excavations have uncovered evidence of partial reconstruction activity afterward. The modern city of Mosul sits directly across the Tigris from the ancient site.

---

## 8. Habakkuk (c. 609-598 BCE)

**Background.** Unique among the Minor Prophets in that his book is entirely a dialogue between prophet and God rather than a proclamation to the people. His central question -- "Why does God use an even more evil nation to punish Israel?" -- made it a touchstone for theodicy.

### Key Prophecies

- The Chaldeans will arise as God's instrument of judgment (1:5-11) -- fulfilled with the Babylonian invasions.
- Five "Woe" oracles against Babylon (2:6-20).
- **Habakkuk 2:4** -- "The righteous shall live by his faith." Cited by Paul in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11.
- **Habakkuk 2:14** -- "The earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea."

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- The righteous are defined by faithfulness (*emunah*) -- loyal trust and patient endurance.
- Condemnation of economic exploitation, judicial corruption, and imperial violence.
- "The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth keep silence before him" (2:20).

### Religious Influence

**The most far-reaching influence of any Minor Prophet on Christian theology.** Habakkuk 2:4 became the cornerstone of Luther's Reformation theology and the doctrine of justification by faith alone (*sola fide*). The Dead Sea Scrolls contain a commentary (pesher) on Habakkuk.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Habakkuk 2:14** -- Universal knowledge of God's glory has not been realized. Listed by jewishroots.net as one of the clearest unfulfilled OT prophecies.

---

## 9. Zephaniah (c. 640-609 BCE)

**Background.** Prophesied during Josiah's reign. His genealogy traced to Hezekiah makes him the only Minor Prophet possibly of royal blood. His message: total cosmic annihilation followed by restoration.

### Key Prophecies

- Destruction of "everything from the face of the earth" -- humans, animals, birds, fish (1:2-3).
- Nineveh shall become a desolation (2:13-15) -- fulfilled 612 BCE.
- **Zephaniah 3:9** -- God will give all peoples "purified lips" to call upon the LORD "shoulder to shoulder."
- Restoration of Zion: God will dwell in Israel's midst and gather the outcast (3:14-20).

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Seek righteousness and humility (2:3).
- Rejection of syncretism, half-hearted worship, and indifference.
- The remnant "shall not do injustice and speak no lies" (3:13).

### Religious Influence

Zephaniah's Day of the LORD directly influenced the medieval Latin hymn *Dies Irae* ("Day of Wrath"), used in Catholic Requiem masses for centuries. His concept of the faithful "remnant" shaped post-exilic Jewish identity and early Christian self-understanding.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Zephaniah 3:9** -- All nations calling on God in unified worship has not occurred. Listed among unfulfilled prophecies by jewishroots.net.
- **Zephaniah 3:14-20** -- Total restoration of Zion with God dwelling physically in Israel's midst remains unfulfilled.

---

## 10. Haggai (520 BCE)

**Background.** One of the most precisely dated prophets. All four oracles fall within 520 BCE. His message: complete the Second Temple.

### Key Prophecies

- Economic hardship is punishment for neglecting the temple (1:5-11).
- **Haggai 2:6-9** -- "I will shake the heavens and the earth... The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."
- **Haggai 2:20-23** -- God will overthrow kingdoms and make Zerubbabel "like a signet ring."

### Religious Influence

His success was immediate: the community resumed temple construction within weeks, and the Second Temple was completed in 516 BCE. The Zerubbabel-as-signet-ring prophecy had enormous influence on messianic expectation.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Haggai 2:6-9 -- "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."** This is arguably the most directly challenged prophecy in all the Minor Prophets. The Second Temple never exceeded the glory of Solomon's temple. The Talmud (Yoma 21b) explicitly lists five things present in the First Temple absent from the Second (including the Ark of the Covenant and the Shekinah glory). The temple was ultimately destroyed by Rome in 70 CE.
- **Haggai 2:20-23 -- Zerubbabel.** He disappears from history without fulfilling any messianic role. He is not recorded as having overthrown any kingdoms.

---

## 11. Zechariah (520-480 BCE)

**Background.** Contemporary of Haggai. Most scholars posit at least two authors: "First Zechariah" (ch. 1-8, 520-518 BCE) and "Second Zechariah" (ch. 9-14, possibly 5th-4th century BCE).

### Key Prophecies

- Eight night visions covering Israel's restoration and the coming of "the Branch" (ch. 1-6).
- **Zechariah 9:9** -- The triumphant king enters Jerusalem on a donkey. Applied to Jesus's Palm Sunday entry.
- **Zechariah 11:12-13** -- The shepherd valued at 30 pieces of silver. Applied to Judas's betrayal.
- **Zechariah 12:10** -- "They will look on me, the one they have pierced." Applied to Jesus's crucifixion (John 19:37).
- **Zechariah 13:7** -- The shepherd struck, the sheep scattered. Cited by Jesus at Gethsemane.
- **Zechariah 14** -- The Mount of Olives split in two; living waters flowing from Jerusalem; the LORD as king over all the earth.

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- "Render true judgments, show kindness and mercy to one another, do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the sojourner, or the poor" (7:9-10).
- Fasting must translate into justice for the vulnerable (7:5-10).

### Religious Influence

Contains more messianic prophecies applied to Jesus in the NT than any other Minor Prophet. "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD" (4:6) is one of the most cited principles in evangelical Christianity.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Zechariah 14:1-11** -- The literal splitting of the Mount of Olives, living waters flowing east and west. None of these have occurred. Martin Luther himself said he could not understand this chapter.
- **Zechariah 14:16-19** -- All surviving nations coming to Jerusalem annually for the Feast of Tabernacles. Never occurred.
- **Zechariah 13:8-9** -- Two-thirds of the Jewish population perishing. Has not happened in any single event matching Zechariah's framework.

---

## 12. Malachi (c. 450-430 BCE)

**Background.** "Malachi" means "my messenger" in Hebrew and is almost certainly a title, not a name. The last book in both the Hebrew and Christian OT canons.

### Key Prophecies

- **Malachi 3:1** -- "I send my messenger to prepare the way before me. And the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple." Christians apply "my messenger" to John the Baptist.
- **Malachi 3:10** -- "Bring the full tithe into the storehouse... if I will not open the windows of heaven for you."
- **Malachi 4:5-6** -- "I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the LORD."

### Rules and Moral Prescriptions

- Fidelity in marriage; prohibition on divorce and taking foreign wives.
- Honest and complete tithing.
- Offering only unblemished animals in worship.
- Justice for the poor, widows, and strangers.

### Religious Influence

- **Judaism:** Malachi is "the seal of the prophets" -- the close of the prophetic era.
- **Christianity:** Malachi 3:10 is perhaps the most preached tithing text in Protestant Christianity. The "Elijah" prophecy profoundly shaped the framework for understanding John the Baptist.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

- **Malachi 4:5-6 -- The Return of Elijah.** Jesus stated John the Baptist was "the Elijah to come" (Matthew 11:14), but John himself denied being Elijah (John 1:21). This paradox has never been satisfactorily resolved.
- **Malachi 3:1-4** -- The Lord purifying the sons of Levi so offerings are "pleasing to the LORD as in the days of old." The temple cult was not purified but was ultimately destroyed in 70 CE.

---

# Part III: Prophetic Figures in Apocryphal and Deuterocanonical Texts

### Note on Canonical Status

- **Catholic & Eastern Orthodox Churches:** Accept Tobit, Baruch, Sirach, and Wisdom of Solomon as canonical Scripture.
- **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church:** Accept the largest canon in Christianity (81+ books), including 1 Enoch and Jubilees.
- **Protestant Churches:** Treat all these as Apocrypha -- useful but non-authoritative.
- **Rabbinic Judaism:** Rejects all of them, though many circulated widely in Second Temple Judaism.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls Community (Qumran):** Evidently revered 1 Enoch and Jubilees as authoritative.

---

## 1 Enoch (Book of Enoch)

**Date:** Earliest sections 3rd century BCE; composed over centuries. Complete text preserved in Ge'ez (Ethiopic); Aramaic fragments found at Qumran.

### Key Prophecies

- **The Watchers (ch. 1-36):** Fallen angels led by Shemihazah and Azazel descend, take human wives, beget the Nephilim, and teach humanity forbidden arts. The Watchers are bound until final judgment.
- **The Apocalypse of Weeks (ch. 91, 93):** History mapped across ten "weeks." Weeks 8-10 project into the eschatological future: triumph of the righteous, dissolution of wickedness, final judgment, and a "new heaven."
- **The Son of Man / Parables (ch. 37-71):** A pre-existent Messianic figure called "Son of Man," "Chosen One," and "Anointed" sits on a throne and judges humanity. In chapter 71, Enoch himself may be identified with this figure.
- **The Animal Apocalypse (ch. 85-90):** All Israelite history narrated through animal symbolism, culminating in a great white bull (messianic figure) renewing creation.

### Laws Prescribed

Condemnation of the forbidden arts the Watchers taught. The Astronomical Book prescribes a 364-day solar calendar, which put Qumran at odds with the Temple establishment.

### Religious Influence

Jude 1:14-15 directly quotes 1 Enoch 1:9. The "Son of Man" concept likely entered Jewish and Christian thought partly through the Parables. Deep influence on NT demonology and eschatology.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

The ten-week schema has not concluded. The Son of Man's final judgment, the binding of the Watchers for release, and the "new heaven" all remain unfulfilled. The specific timeline does not map cleanly onto any historical system.

---

## 2 Esdras (4 Ezra)

**Date:** Core written c. 100 CE, after the Second Temple's destruction.

### Key Prophecies

- **The 400-Year Messianic Reign (7:26-32):** An earthly Messiah reigns for 400 years, then dies along with all humanity, followed by primeval silence, general resurrection, and final judgment. No such reign has been identified.
- **The Eagle Vision (ch. 11-12):** The Roman Empire as a massive eagle -- explicitly identified. A lion (the Davidic Messiah) condemns and destroys Rome. Rome did not fall to a Messiah.
- **The Man from the Sea (ch. 13):** A pre-existent figure destroying hostile nations with fire from his mouth.
- **Ezra's Lost Scriptures (ch. 14):** 94 books dictated -- 24 public (the Hebrew Bible) and 70 esoteric.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

The 400-year messianic reign, the messianic destruction of Rome, the gathering of the ten lost tribes, and the resurrection of all the dead have not occurred.

---

## Baruch and 2 Baruch

### Book of Baruch (Deuterocanonical)

**Date:** Late 2nd or early 1st century BCE. Canonical in Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches.

Baruch 4-5 contains prophecies of Jerusalem's restoration and the humbling of the nations that oppressed her. Chapters 3-4 equate Wisdom with Torah. Baruch 3:37-38 ("Afterward she appeared on earth and lived among mortals") was read by Church Fathers as a prophecy of the Incarnation.

### 2 Baruch (Syriac Apocalypse)

**Date:** c. 100-130 CE, in response to the 70 CE destruction.

- **Vision of Twelve Waters:** Alternating dark and bright waters symbolize world history from Adam to the Messiah. After the twelve comes a final fiery water, then a clear flash -- the messianic age.
- Zion will be rebuilt for all eternity. The Manna will descend again. Behemoth and Leviathan will be eaten by the saints.
- **Unfulfilled:** The eternal Zion, the descent of manna, and the cosmic feast remain unfulfilled.

---

## Tobit

**Date:** 3rd to early 2nd century BCE. Found at Qumran. Canonical in Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches.

### Key Prophetic Elements

- **Tobit's Deathbed Prophecy (ch. 14):** Nineveh will be destroyed (historically fulfilled 612 BCE). The Temple will be destroyed and rebuilt. All Gentiles will turn to fear the Lord.
- **Tobit's Hymn (ch. 13):** Jerusalem's eschatological restoration; all nations will come to worship.

### Unfulfilled Elements

The conversion of all Gentiles and the permanent rebuilding of Jerusalem in transcendent splendor remain unfulfilled.

---

## Sirach (Ecclesiasticus)

**Date:** c. 196-175 BCE, by Yeshua ben Sira. Canonical in Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches.

### Prophetic Content

- **Praise of Famous Men (ch. 44-49):** Catalogs Israel's prophets and leaders, including the earliest clear canonical listing of the Twelve Minor Prophets as a unified collection (49:10).
- **Sirach 36:** A prayer for Israel's liberation, the gathering of the tribes, and the fulfillment of ancient prophecies.
- Sirach equates wisdom with Torah observance and the prophetic tradition.

### Laws Prescribed

Strict Sabbath observance, care for the poor, filial piety, honest business, proper sacrifice, avoidance of idolatry, care in speech, and hospitality.

---

## Wisdom of Solomon

**Date:** Late 1st century BCE to early 1st century CE. Canonical in Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches.

### Key Prophetic Themes

- **Wisdom 2:12-20 -- The Suffering Righteous Man.** The wicked plot against one who "calls himself a child of the Lord" and claims knowledge of God. They sentence him to a shameful death. Read by Christians from earliest times as a prophecy of Jesus's passion. Matthew's Gospel appears to allude to it in shaping the passion narrative. However, scholars debate whether this is formal prophecy or a typological portrait drawn from Isaiah's Servant Songs.
- **Immortality of the Soul (ch. 3):** "The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and no torment will ever touch them." A revolutionary integration of Platonic immortality with Jewish faith.

---

## Jubilees

**Date:** 160-150 BCE. Canonical only in Ethiopian Orthodox tradition. Fragments found at Qumran.

### Prophetic Content

Framed as a revelation from the Angel of the Presence to Moses on Sinai. History is divided into 49-year Jubilee periods. Predicts repeated cycles of apostasy, punishment, and restoration, culminating in a final age when Israel keeps Torah perfectly.

### Laws Prescribed

Jubilees is intensely law-focused and often stricter than canonical Torah:
- 364-day solar calendar (opposed to the Temple's lunar calendar) -- a major sectarian issue at Qumran.
- Strict Sabbath laws: any work punishable by death.
- Strict endogamy (marriage within Israel only).
- Prohibition on eating blood.
- Circumcision on the eighth day.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

The perfect eschatological Israel keeping all Jubilees' laws has not materialized. The precise chronological schema does not align with external history.

---

## Assumption / Testament of Moses

**Date:** Early 1st century CE; single incomplete 6th-century Latin manuscript.

### Key Prophecies

Moses recounts Israel's entire future to Joshua: the conquest, monarchy, exile, Hasmonean dynasty, Herod the Great and his sons (clearly identified in ch. 6), and Roman persecution.

- **Taxo and His Seven Sons (ch. 9):** A Levite named Taxo retreats to a cave to die rather than sin, meant to trigger divine intervention.
- **Eschatological Hymn (ch. 10):** God will arise, punish the Gentiles, destroy Satan, and exalt Israel to the stars.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

The cosmic intervention, destruction of Satan, and permanent exaltation of Israel did not occur at the end of the Herodian period when the text expected it.

---

## Ascension of Isaiah

**Date:** Composite text, 3rd-4th century CE, from three distinct earlier layers.

### Key Prophecies

- **Martyrdom of Isaiah (ch. 1-5):** Isaiah sawn in two under Manasseh (cf. Hebrews 11:37).
- **Testament of Hezekiah (ch. 3:13-4:22):** Beliar (Satan) incarnate as the dead Nero risen (*Nero redivivus* myth). Beliar/Nero reigns for 1,332 days before the Lord descends in final judgment.
- **Vision of Isaiah (ch. 6-11):** Isaiah ascends through seven heavens and witnesses the entire incarnation, passion, resurrection, and ascension of Christ before it happens.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

Nero did not return from death. The Parousia did not follow Nero's fall. The 1,332-day tribulation under Nero-as-Beliar did not occur.

---

## Psalms of Solomon

**Date:** 1st century BCE, by a Pharisaic group responding to Pompey's conquest of Jerusalem (63 BCE).

### Key Prophecies

- **Psalm 17 -- The Messianic King:** A "Son of David" who will purge Jerusalem of Gentiles, gather the scattered tribes, rule with wisdom and righteousness, and receive submission from all nations. "Their king shall be the Lord Messiah" (*christos kurios*). Echoed in Luke 2:11.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

The promised Davidic warrior-king who would drive out the Romans, gather all 12 tribes, and reign from Jerusalem did not appear in the form envisioned.

---

## Sibylline Oracles

**Date:** 150 BCE to 180 CE, by various Jewish and Christian authors imitating Greco-Roman Sibylline prophecy.

### Key Prophecies

- **Book 3 (Jewish, c. 150 BCE):** Universal peace under a divine king from the sun; rebuilding of the Temple as the universal center of worship; conversion of all nations.
- **Books 4-5 (post-70 CE):** Nero's return from the East to lead cosmic warfare against Rome before final judgment.
- **Book 8:** JESUS CHRIST acrostic poem embedded as prophetic sign.

### Unfulfilled Prophecies

Nero never returned. Rome was not destroyed by divine fire. Universal monotheistic conversion did not occur. The rebuilt Temple as world center has not materialized.

---

## Lives of the Prophets

**Date:** 1st century CE. Not considered scripture by any tradition.

Short biographical sketches of 23 prophetic figures, covering birthplace, manner of death, burial site, and legendary traditions. Isaiah sawn in two, Jeremiah stoned in Egypt, Ezekiel martyred in Babylon. The tradition of prophetic martyrdom was crucial to early Christian self-understanding (cf. Matthew 5:12).

# Part IV: Cross-Cutting Analysis

---

## Frameworks for Handling Unfulfilled Prophecy

Scholars and theologians have developed several frameworks:

1. **Conditional Prophecy.** Jeremiah 18:7-10 explicitly states prophecies can be conditional on human behavior. If the people repent, doom is averted.

2. **Partial/Progressive Fulfillment.** A prophecy may be partially fulfilled in the near term, with complete fulfillment awaited in the future.

3. **Typological Fulfillment.** An event is a "type" or foreshadowing of a greater antitype, without requiring literal point-by-point correspondence.

4. **Prophetic Foreshortening.** Prophets collapsed near and distant events into a single panoramic vision without signaling the gap between them.

5. **Vaticinium ex Eventu.** Critical scholars argue some books (especially Daniel, portions of Nahum) were written after the events they purport to predict -- "prophecy after the fact."

6. **Prophets as Reformers, Not Predictors.** A major scholarly trend argues prophets were primarily moral reformers. The purpose was to call communities to faithfulness, not to produce a schedule of future events.

---

## The Day of the LORD Across All Prophets

Almost every prophet -- from Amos (who invented or radically redefined the concept) through Malachi -- contains some version of the Day of the LORD: a cosmic day of divine judgment affecting all nations, all nature, and the entire cosmos. By definition, this universal transformation has not occurred in any historical event, making it the single largest category of unfulfilled prophetic prediction.

| Prophet | Day of the LORD Content |
|---------|------------------------|
| Amos | Darkness, not light (5:18-20) |
| Joel | Sun to darkness, moon to blood (2:30-31) |
| Obadiah | Judgment on all nations (v. 15) |
| Zephaniah | Total cosmic annihilation (1:2-3) |
| Habakkuk | Earth filled with God's glory (2:14) |
| Zechariah | Mount of Olives split, living waters (ch. 14) |
| Malachi | Refiner's fire, Elijah's return (3:2; 4:5) |
| Isaiah | New heavens and new earth (65:17) |
| Ezekiel | Gog and Magog war (38-39) |
| Daniel | Resurrection of the dead (12:2) |

---

## The Enduring Ethical Legacy

Despite intense debate over predictive elements, the ethical legacy of the prophets has been immense:

- **Amos and Micah** pioneered the equation of true religion with social justice.
- **Hosea** pioneered the theology of divine love and covenant faithfulness.
- **Habakkuk 2:4** launched the Protestant Reformation.
- **Malachi 3:10** continues to shape institutional religious practice.
- **Micah 4:3** inspired the United Nations "swords into plowshares" sculpture.
- **Joel 2:28** shaped charismatic Christianity.
- **Isaiah 58** defined true fasting as justice for the oppressed.
- **Jeremiah 31** gave us the concept of the "New Covenant."
- **Ezekiel 18** established individual moral responsibility.
- **Micah 6:8** remains the most compressed ethical statement in the Hebrew Bible.

## How Apocryphal Texts Expand Canonical Prophecy

| Text | Primary Canonical Sources Expanded | Nature of Expansion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Enoch | Genesis 6 (sons of God), Daniel 7 (Son of Man) | Cosmic mythology, angelic hierarchies, pre-existent Messiah |
| 2 Esdras | Daniel (four kingdoms), Jeremiah (Babylon) | Refocuses four kingdoms onto Rome; adds 400-year messianic reign |
| Baruch | Jeremiah (exile), Isaiah (restoration) | Consolidates exile/restoration; equates Wisdom with Torah |
| 2 Baruch | Baruch + Daniel | Twelve-waters vision of history; resurrection details |
| Tobit | Nahum (Nineveh), Amos/Isaiah (exile/return) | Embeds prophetic fulfillment into personal narrative |
| Sirach | Proverbs, all prophetic books | Synthesizes wisdom and prophecy; first list of Twelve Prophets |
| Wisdom | Isaiah 52-53 (Servant Songs), Proverbs 8 | Hellenizes suffering servant; personifies divine Wisdom |
| Jubilees | Genesis, Exodus | Reframes narrative as prophetic revelation; imposes strict calendar |
| Testament of Moses | Deuteronomy 31-34 | Extends Moses' farewell into full apocalyptic history |
| Ascension of Isaiah | Isaiah's life | Martyrdom tradition; cosmic descent of Christ narrative |
| Psalms of Solomon | Psalms, Isaiah 11, Ezekiel 34 | Sharpens Davidic messianism against Roman occupation |
| Sibylline Oracles | Daniel, Isaiah | Translates Jewish eschatology into Greco-Roman literary form |

## Patterns in Unfulfilled Prophecy

The most consistently unfulfilled categories across all prophetic literature:

1. **Imminent end-time events.** Nearly every text composed between 150 BCE and 150 CE expected final judgment, the Messiah's reign, or divine intervention in the near term. None occurred within expected timeframes.

2. **The restored Davidic political kingdom.** Expelling all foreign occupiers and establishing a theocratic state ruled from Zion was never accomplished.

3. **Universal Gentile conversion.** All nations worshipping one God in unified practice has not occurred.

4. **Specific geopolitical predictions.** Damascus never permanently destroyed (Isaiah 17). Egypt never desolated for 40 years (Ezekiel 29). Tyre exists today as Sur, Lebanon (Ezekiel 26). Nero never returned from death (Sibylline Oracles).

5. **Cosmic transformation.** The new heavens and new earth, the resurrection of all the dead, the abolition of death and mourning -- the universal transformation described by virtually every prophet -- remains the defining unfulfilled promise of prophetic literature.

6. **The Second Temple surpassing the First.** Haggai's specific prediction is contradicted by the Talmud's own accounting of what the Second Temple lacked.
